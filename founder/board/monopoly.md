# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The buyer group is real and countable: IBISWorld counts 618 interurban and rural bus businesses; BusNSW about 950 accredited NSW operators. A small market is the right kind of start for the Monopoly lens.
- Compliance is a possible wedge. NSW accreditation and the BOAS audit regime (tool updated October 2025) is a recurring, rule-driven pain.
- Switching costs, once won, would be high: fleet, driver and maintenance history is sticky data.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Crowded market, incremental product.** Coach Manager, eCoachManager, TransportMe (claims 150+ companies, 3,500+ vehicles), Busable and others already sell to the same operators. The feature list matches theirs; nothing in the brief is ten times better on anything that matters. This is competition by design.
2. **No distribution.** Sales channel undecided; no interviews, letters of intent or customers. Small operators are fragmented and hard to reach. With no plan for how the first 100 customers arrive or what each costs, this is a product idea, not yet a business.
3. **No secret, no durability.** The brief names nothing the founder knows that incumbents do not, and no network effect, scale advantage or brand. Pricing (A$199–699/month) sits above Coach Manager's published A$137.50–385, so it is neither cheaper nor yet better. An incumbent adding one feature closes any gap by month six.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which small segment could you own outright in 18 months (e.g. NSW charter operators facing BOAS audits), and how many businesses is that?
- What do you know about these operators that the incumbents have missed, and where did you learn it?
- What would be ten times better than today's option, and how would an operator see it in week one?
- How do the first 100 customers arrive (association, auditor, telematics partner, direct sales), and what does each cost? (Open question: no acquisition cost or conversion figure exists.)
- Why now? What has changed that opens a gap today?
- Why would an operator leave an incumbent, and what does switching cost them?
- Is a network effect within reach, e.g. linking operators, schools, councils or charter brokers on one booking layer?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable: the vote is PASS. The lens would look again given a named niche, a secret that interviews back up, and a tested channel with real early customers.
